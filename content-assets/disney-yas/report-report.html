Disney Is Coming To Yas Island - Here's What It Did Everywhere Else

Calum MacLeod · 13 November 2026 · all figures in US dollars, indicative

Why I wrote this

Disney has opened six resorts in 70 years. Near the ones that worked, property moved most between the announcement and the opening, not after it. Disneyland Abu Dhabi was announced in May 2025 and is expected in the early 2030s, so Yas is in that window now.

Before the next phase of Yas Point launches on 26 November, I wanted to see the evidence for myself: what happened near every Disney, and where Yas stands today. Everything is in US dollars. I've also kept in the parts that don't flatter the story, because you should see those too.

What Disney did to property

Three resorts have the price data to show it clearly: Orlando, Shanghai and Paris.

Orlando. Walt Disney bought 27,000 acres of Florida swamp in secret, paying under $200 an acre. Once the 1965 announcement broke, an acre nearby sold for $131,000. By the time the park opened in 1971, land was trading at up to 500 times its 1965 price.

Shanghai. The clearest before-and-after of all. Homes near the site jumped about fivefold in the two to three years around the 2009 approval, and 90 minutes after the green light a nearby plot sold for 264% of its starting price. Over 2009-2015, homes near Disney rose 162%, against 101% across Shanghai.

Paris. Chessy was a farming village when Disney arrived in 1992. Today, apartments in the two towns at Disney's gates sell at a 41-47% premium to the rest of the département, 34 years on.

The other three, in a line each:

  • Anaheim, 1955: a town of about 14,500 in 1950 had over 104,000 people by 1960, with 25 hotels around the park. The post-war boom helped; Disney accelerated it.
  • Tokyo, 1983: built on land reclaimed from the sea by a local company that licenses the Disney name. The town around it nearly doubled in the decade it opened.
  • Hong Kong, 2005: small, isolated, and lost money in eight of its first eleven years. I couldn't find any property boom around it.

The pattern

The big moves came in the years between the announcement and the opening. In Orlando and Shanghai, land and homes jumped on the news, years before a single ride ran.

It isn't automatic. Hong Kong shows that Disney on its own doesn't move a market: size, location and the wider economy matter as much.

Yas today

Yas is already full of visitors without Disney, and it's the best-connected island to Dubai. But so far, its prices have risen with Abu Dhabi, not ahead of it.

Demand, before Disney. Yas hotels averaged 85% occupancy over summer 2025. In August 2026, the year of the regional conflict, they were 92% full. August is the hottest, quietest month of the UAE's tourist year, and Yas fills up anyway, because its theme parks are indoors.

Visitors. Yas had 44 million visits in 2025, up 16% on the year before. Abu Dhabi's tourism plan targets 39.3 million visitors a year to the emirate by 2030, and 50,000 hotel rooms. Yas counts every trip, residents included, so the two aren't like for like, but the direction is clear.

Closest to Dubai, and the rail. Yas is the Abu Dhabi island nearest Dubai, about 50 minutes by road. The bigger change is the high-speed rail, now under construction, with a station on Yas: up to 350 km/h, and about 30 minutes to Dubai. One line, six stops: Reem, Saadiyat, Yas and Zayed International Airport in Abu Dhabi, then Al Maktoum Airport and Al Jaddaf in Dubai. The contractor is targeting 2031 (Etihad Rail had said 2030), the same year the Canopies Sanctuary hands over and just ahead of Disney. Dubai's residents and visitors end up half an hour from Yas, with Abu Dhabi's airport one stop away.

Since the announcement. Comparing the same buildings in the year before May 2025 and the last twelve months, Yas resale prices are up 33%. Reem, Al Raha and Saadiyat moved by similar amounts. On the Shanghai pattern, the Disney premium hasn't shown up on Yas yet.

What it could mean for Yas

This is an illustration from the one comparable case, not a forecast. Shanghai is the only Disney with price data both next to the park and across the whole city, and by opening, homes near Disney ended about 30% above where the city's growth alone would have taken them.

  • Yas's top new buildings today: about $720-800 per sq ft
  • With a Shanghai-style 30% premium: about $940-1,040 per sq ft, in today's money, before any general market growth
  • On Hong Kong's path: no premium at all

That upper figure isn't a stretch: Saadiyat's newest launches already sell at $940-1,060 per sq ft.

What could go wrong

  • Yas hasn't beaten the market yet. Since the announcement it has moved with Reem, Al Raha and Saadiyat, not ahead of them. The premium may come, or may be smaller than Shanghai's.
  • Supply. Around 7,700 homes are under construction on Yas, more than on any other Abu Dhabi island.
  • Handover waves. When many units hand over at once, resale prices can pause for a while. Yas Point will hand over a large phase in one go too.
  • No opening date. Disney's own estimate is one to two years to design and four to six to build, which points to the early 2030s. Timelines slip.
  • Shanghai cooled. The frenzy around the 2009 approval later settled back to the city's average growth.
  • It isn't automatic. Hong Kong had Disney and no premium.
