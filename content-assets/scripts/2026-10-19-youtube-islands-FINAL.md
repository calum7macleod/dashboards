FIRST, WHY NOW

Three things explain why Abu Dhabi is happening now. The money, the law, and the train.

Start with the money. Abu Dhabi's government has around one point nine trillion dollars in sovereign wealth funds - more per person than any country on earth. Those funds own the developers. Modon, the company building Hudayriyat, is eighty-five per cent owned by one of them. Aldar, the company building Saadiyat and Yas, is twenty-eight per cent owned by another. So when you hear a developer's name in this video, there's a government fund behind it.

The money works from one place - ADGM, Abu Dhabi Global Market, the financial district on Al Maryah Island. It runs on English law. BlackRock, Goldman Sachs and Binance are there. Brevan Howard moved in 2023 and now runs more money from Abu Dhabi than from anywhere in the world. The workforce grew thirty-four per cent in a year, to forty-nine thousand people, and every one of them needs somewhere to live. That's why I'll tell you how far each island is from ADGM - it's the number that decides who wants to rent there.

Then the law. Freehold means you own the land and the building outright, forever. Dubai opened it to foreigners in 2002. Abu Dhabi didn't until 2019, so this market is seven years old. England created freehold in 1290, and seven years later London was one square mile with a hundred thousand people. That's where Abu Dhabi is on the timeline - and prices here still rose eighteen per cent in the last twelve months.

And the train. From 2031, high-speed rail runs between Abu Dhabi and Dubai at three hundred and fifty kilometres an hour, thirty minutes end to end. So I'll also tell you whether the train stops on each island, because from 2031 that decides which islands are half an hour from Dubai and which aren't.

Money, law, train. Now the islands - starting with the one where I put my own money.

SECOND, THE ISLANDS EVERYONE'S HEARD OF. REEM AND AL MARYAH

Second, the islands everyone's heard of - starting with the one where I put my own money.

Reem and Al Maryah are two islands, both inside ADGM. Maryah is the financial district itself - the towers, the Galleria, Cleveland Clinic. Reem, next door, is where most of the people who work there live.

In April 2026 I bought a two-bedroom apartment on Reem - Tara Park, by Modon, six hundred and eighty thousand dollars, a mile from ADGM. The building sold out, over half a billion dollars of apartments in total. So I'm not neutral about Reem, and I'll give you what's wrong with it as well.

Here's what drew me. The 2024 census counted fifty-nine thousand seven hundred people living on Reem, up eight per cent in a year. Apartment prices rose eighteen per cent in the year to June 2026. Prices on Reem are around five hundred and fifty dollars a square foot - Tara Park was just under that. On Maryah, the new branded launches like the Ritz-Carlton Residences are around eleven to twelve hundred. Yields on Reem are six to eight per cent, the highest of the big islands. Launches are typically forty per cent during construction, sixty on handover, with handovers around 2029 to 2030.

Next door, in December 2025, Mubadala and Aldar announced a sixteen billion dollar expansion of Maryah - half a million square metres of office, three thousand homes, and the bridges go in first.

Reem is part of ADGM, so there's no distance to measure. There'll be a high-speed rail stop here too - and for this stretch it's likely to be underground; most of the line is, until it gets near the airport.

Reem has more tenants per square foot than anywhere in Abu Dhabi, because it's where the financial district lives.

Across the bridge is the most expensive island.

SAADIYAT

Saadiyat is a natural island, twenty-seven square kilometres, with nine kilometres of protected beach that turtles nest on and dolphins swim off. It's been voted the best beach in the Middle East twelve years running.

It's Abu Dhabi's cultural district. The Louvre is here - one point four million visitors in 2024. The Zayed National Museum opened in December 2025. And the Guggenheim - the largest building Frank Gehry has ever designed - has a December 2026 opening date, twenty years after it was announced.

If you want to know what that does to property, look at Bilbao. When Gehry's Guggenheim opened there in 1997, property nearby rose thirty per cent in five years, and the museum paid for itself within seven. Economists call it the Guggenheim effect. Saadiyat already has three museums. The Guggenheim makes four.

Prices for apartments are around eleven hundred dollars a square foot - the highest in Abu Dhabi, and yields are around four per cent, the lowest. Prices rose twenty-one per cent in the year to June 2026. The record sale is thirty-five million dollars, for one villa.

Marsa Al Saadiyat launched in July 2026 - a twenty-seven billion dollar district with its own marina and its own rail station, villas from three point seven million dollars, around seven hundred dollars a square foot - and it's the last waterfront on the island. Recent launches here have been on a fifty-fifty plan, with handover at the end of 2029. After Marsa, there's no more land.

Saadiyat is ten minutes from ADGM by road, and it gets its own stop on the high-speed line - so from 2031 it's also forty minutes from Dubai's financial centre.

What's wrong with it is the flip side of all that. It's the dearest island by a distance, so it has the least room to rise from here. And no more land protects what's already there - but it caps what's coming too.

Saadiyat is the island people hold on to, not the one they trade.

Next, the island with forty-four million visitors.

YAS

Yas is twenty-five square kilometres, and forty per cent of it is still empty.

On the rest, there's Ferrari World, SeaWorld, Warner Brothers, the Formula One circuit and an eighteen-thousand-seat arena. Forty-four million visits in 2025. Hotel occupancy hit eighty-five per cent in August 2026 - the month the Gulf is supposed to be quiet.

What's coming is bigger than what's there. Disneyland Abu Dhabi, the seventh Disney park in the world, was announced in May 2025 for around 2030 to 2033. A Sphere - the second on earth after Las Vegas - is due in 2029.

The property followed the announcements. Apartment prices rose twenty-four per cent in 2025 on the Disney news, and another eighteen per cent in the year to June 2026. The average sale is five hundred and sixty thousand dollars. When Aldar launched Yas Point in July 2026 it took four hundred million dollars in days, and eighty-four per cent of the people who bought had never bought from Aldar before. Prices are around six hundred and fifty dollars a square foot, and yields around six per cent. Plans run from thirty to fifty-five per cent during construction depending on the project, with handovers between 2027 and 2030.

Yas is twenty-five minutes from ADGM. The train stops here, and it's the closest island to Dubai.

What's wrong with it is supply. Seven thousand seven hundred homes are under construction on Yas right now - more than on any other island. Forty-four million visitors on one side, seven thousand seven hundred new homes on the other. Whether that balances is the question for the next five years.

Yas is the holiday island. Apartments here rent to tourists, not to bankers.

Hudayriyat is fifty-three million square metres - the same size as Palm Jumeirah, Dubai Marina, JBR, Downtown, Business Bay, Dubai Hills, Jumeirah Golf Estates, Arabian Ranches and Emirates Hills put together. The whole island has one developer, Modon. In the first half of 2026, Modon sold six point three billion dollars of property, more than any developer in the UAE, including Emaar.

In July 2026, Modon launched Hudayriyat Golf Estates. Seventeen hundred homes, three and a half billion dollars, sold in days - the biggest single property launch in the history of the UAE. Fifteen per cent of the people who bought lived outside the country. Eighty-one per cent had never bought from Modon before.

It wasn't a one-off. Wadeem - seventeen hundred plots - sold out in seventy-two hours. Nawayef Village - over half a billion dollars of townhouses - went in hours.

Here's what it's for. Hudayriyat is twelve kilometres from ADGM, twenty minutes by road. Reem has a few villas, but not the hills, the beaches or the golf. Saadiyat can, at eleven hundred dollars a foot. Hudayriyat is twenty minutes from ADGM by road.

THIRD, THE ONES MOST PEOPLE HAVEN'T. JUBAIL

Third, the ones most people haven't heard of.

Jubail sits between Yas and Saadiyat, inside forty square kilometres of mangroves. It's low-density villas in six villages, with over a thousand homes already handed over, and in 2026 Gordonstoun - the school the British King went to - opens a campus here.

Prices are around six hundred dollars a square foot for a villa, and most of what's released is already built. It's twenty minutes from ADGM, with no train. What's wrong with it is that it's a quiet market - fewer sales, so slower to get out of.

Jubail is the island families choose for the nature and the schools - and a thousand homes are already lived in, which none of the next three can say.

FAHID

Fahid is Aldar's newest island, next to Yas. Two point seven million square metres, eleven billion dollars, six thousand homes planned. It's the first island in the world certified as a healthy place to live - eleven kilometres of coast, a ten-kilometre fitness trail, a third of it green. Studios start at four hundred and ninety thousand dollars, and King's College Wimbledon opens a school here in 2028. Prices are around a thousand dollars a square foot - new beachfront. No yields yet. Plans are sixty-five per cent during construction, thirty-five on handover, with handovers in 2029.

It's five minutes from Yas, twenty-five from ADGM, with no train. What's wrong with it is that nothing has handed over yet - the first homes come in 2029.

Fahid is Yas, but quieter.

RAMHAN

Ramhan is between Yas and Saadiyat - four million square metres, with around thirteen hundred and fifty beach villas and nine hundred marina apartments planned, plus a hotel and a yacht club. Villas start at one point seven five million dollars, the show villas are open now. Prices are around five hundred and fifty dollars a square foot at launch, with resales already asking well above that. No yields yet. Handovers are 2027 for the first villas and 2028 for the first apartments. It's by Eagle Hills, founded by Mohamed Alabbar - the man who founded Emaar, the company behind the Burj Khalifa, Dubai Mall and Downtown Dubai.

It's twenty-five minutes from ADGM, with no train. What's wrong with it is that it's the most isolated of the ten, from a developer that's new to Abu Dhabi.

Ramhan is the one you choose for the water - a villa on a beach or an apartment on a marina, and the sea is the whole point.

BVLGARI

Bvlgari Island is near the Corniche, also by Eagle Hills. Sixty hotel rooms, thirty beach villas, ninety mansions and a yacht club, opening in 2030, designed with the jeweller itself.

What's wrong with it is that it isn't really a market - it's a guest list.

Bvlgari is built for the world's elite, and the ninety mansions are sold by invitation.

And the last one.

LULU

Lulu Island is four point two million square metres - the size of Palm Jumeirah - five hundred metres off the Corniche. It's the best-located island in the city, and for thirty-four years there's been nothing on it.

It was built in the eighties as a breakwater. It opened to the public in 1992 with a ferry and restaurants. Two masterplans have been announced since, and both failed. Abu Dhabi's own 2030 plan calls it the most important element of the capital's framework.

In August 2026, Eagle Hills confirmed it's developing it. You can see the cranes from the Corniche, and bridges to the city are planned.

What's wrong with it is that there's no masterplan, no prices and no date.

Lulu is the most central new address Abu Dhabi will ever launch. When, nobody knows.
