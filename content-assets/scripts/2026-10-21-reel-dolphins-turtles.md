# Why Dolphins And Turtles Choose Abu Dhabi Over Dubai - reel script (Calum, 21 Oct 2026)

Text: Why Dolphins And Turtles Choose Abu Dhabi Over Dubai
Speech: Eight thousand turtles and the world's biggest humpback dolphin population swim in Abu Dhabi, here's why

Four reasons - I've never heard anyone mention the last one before

One. Mangroves
- Abu Dhabi has 176 square kilometres of mangroves - up 35% in a decade
- They purify the water
- Young fish and crabs live in mangrove roots - perfect for hungry dolphins and turtles

Two. Islands and shallow water
- 214 islands break the coast into lagoons a few metres deep - warm, calm, sheltered from the open sea
- In 2007 Abu Dhabi put 4,255 square kilometres of that sea under UN protection - Marawah, the largest marine reserve in the Gulf
- Seventeen marine protected areas in total. Dubai's coast is dredged and built out

Three. Nests on Saadiyat
- 9 kilometres of natural beach and dunes, hawksbills nesting every year - critically endangered turtles
- Aldar built a dune protection zone and raised walkways; the hotels turn their lights down in nesting season so baby turtles find the sea

Four. Seagrass
- Abu Dhabi has over 3,000 square kilometres of seagrass - 98% of all the seagrass in the UAE
- It feeds 3,000 dugongs - sea cows, the second-largest population on earth after Australia - and 4,000 green turtles
- No seagrass, no dugongs. Dubai has almost none

Seven hundred humpback dolphins - the largest population on earth. Eight thousand turtles. This is why they chose Abu Dhabi.
