Five things - the last one is a problem

One. Al Naseem
- A 4, 5 or 6 bed standalone villa on a 10 thousand square foot plot, on an island, for the same price as a terraced house in Clapham - I'll tell you the price in a sec
- Here it buys sea air, the world's biggest surf pool at the end of the road, and a velodrome where your kids learn to ride
- That's Al Naseem. Here's the island it sits on

Two. Hudayriyat Island
- 53 million square metres - nearly the size of Manhattan with a ninth of the population density - and 22 kilometres of beach
- The world's biggest wave pool, the Middle East's first Olympic velodrome and the beach clubs are already open - before a single home has
- Two golf courses, sixteen schools and twelve hotels coming
- And the city it belongs to is the reason it's so good

Three. Abu Dhabi
- No income tax. No capital gains tax. One of the world's safest cities
- A 1.9 trillion dollar sovereign fund behind the developer, and a financial district twenty minutes away that grew 34% last year
- It's slower than Dubai, on purpose. Here's why it's so cheap right now

Four. Price
- Al Naseem sold out in January, then every broker moved on to the next launch and the spring conflict broke the thread
- Some owners were sold it as a quick flip by brokers, and the instalment they never planned on paying was due recently
- You can now buy them for close to original price - 2.3 to 3 million dollars depending on unit type
- Just over 1,000 dirhams per square foot. Recent launches have been 1,800 dirhams a foot
- But here's the problem

Five. The catch
- It's resale, not off-plan - the seller has to be 20% paid, so you're bringing up to 20% in cash plus any premium. More up front than a new launch
- You pay a 2% broker fee - for off-plan the developer pays it
- It needs more cash, but you get a bigger property for less money, with an earlier handover

A property with a garden, by the sea, twenty minutes from your desk, in a city with no income tax. Not bad, right?
