Three things, the last is what it does to prices

One. The problem
- Dubai is 140 kilometres away - an hour twenty by road, and the first twenty minutes are getting off the island, over one bridge, through Al Bateen, across the city to the highway
- Every Dubai buyer I speak to says the same thing: it's too far. Here's what's coming

Two. The fix
- Two underwater tunnels from the east of Hudayriyat to Mussafah - 4.8 kilometres, four lanes each way, straight onto the E11. The city's no longer in the way
- Modon Infrastructure is building it with the transport department, 25-year public-private deal, advisers tendered this summer
- Plus two more bridges tendered in January, and a 37-kilometre coastal expressway announced in October running west from the island. One way on today; five by the time the hills hand over
- And the train is the other half

Three. What it does
- Dubai by road drops to around an hour. The high-speed rail from 2031 does it in 30 from the ADGM stop
- In Dubai, every community that got a new road got a price bump - Dubai Hills has become one of Dubai's most valuable addresses since Al Khail opened. Hudayriyat's getting three
- The catch: tunnels take three years to dig, so the "too far" objection stays until about 2029. Which is exactly when the first hills hand over
