Five things, the last one's a potential problem

One. Museums
- The Louvre Abu Dhabi - 1.4 million visitors last year - the Zayed National Museum, the Natural History Museum, all open
- In December 2026 the Guggenheim opens: Frank Gehry's biggest building anywhere, twenty years after it was announced. Four museums in a mile - more than South Kensington
- Here's what that does to prices

Two. Bilbao effect
- In 1997 Bilbao was a declining industrial port - think Glasgow in the eighties. Along came the Guggenheim
- 1.3 million visitors in year one, three times what they'd planned for. The museum paid for itself in tax revenue in seven years [+ property nearby rose 30% in five]. Economists call it the Bilbao effect
- Saadiyat's Guggenheim will be the jewel in the crown, rather than the life raft in the storm it was for Bilbao
- But there's more than just culture

Three. Beach
- Nine kilometres of natural beach - 90 football pitches long - voted the best in the Middle East twelve years running
- Turtles nest on it, dolphins swim off it, and the hotels dim their lights in nesting season
- Which is why it costs what it costs

Four. Numbers
- Apartments around 1,100 dollars a square foot - the most expensive in Abu Dhabi, and about half of Kensington
- Prices up 21% to June 2026. The record sale: a 35 million dollar villa
- Marsa Al Saadiyat, launched in July - a 27 billion dollar district with a marina and high-speed rail stop - is the last waterfront on the island
- And here's the problem

Five. Catch
- The dearest island in the city rents at around 4% - the lowest yield of them all
- No more land protects what's there, and caps what's coming. If you want rental returns, look elsewhere

Saadiyat is the island you hold, not the one you trade. Next time, the island you can't buy on at all. DM ISLAND for the PDF.
