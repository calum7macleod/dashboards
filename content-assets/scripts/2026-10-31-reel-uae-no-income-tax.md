Four things, nobody talks about the last one

One. Oil
- Half of the UAE's 155 billion dollars annual income comes from oil
- ADNOC pumps around 4 million barrels a day; its dividends, royalties and taxes go to the Abu Dhabi government
- Britain had North Sea oil too - it spent the money. Abu Dhabi saved it. Here's what that saving does now

Two. Sovereign wealth funds
- 1.9 trillion dollars across three funds, built from oil since 1967. The returns alone are tens of billions a year, before a barrel is sold
- Norway's fund is bigger. Norway also has 25% VAT and income tax up to 47%. Abu Dhabi has 5% and none
- And since 2018, two more sources

Three. The new taxes
- 5% VAT since 2018, 9% corporate tax since 2023 - on companies, not people. Together roughly a fifth of the income
- Plus fees: visas, licences, the 2% on your property. All of it on transactions, none on earnings
- So does it add up?

Four. The surplus
- The UAE collects about 28% of its economy and spends 23. A surplus, most years. Britain collects 40 and spends 44, borrowing the gap every year
- Only 30 countries in the world are in a surplus, and that number drops every year
- The honest bit: it leans on oil, and oil prices move. That's why the funds exist - and why the 9% company tax arrived
- No income tax is thanks to a 1.9 trillion dollar fund and oil, and will continue for many years to come

The UAE doesn't need your salary because it saved its oil money. Britain needs yours because it didn't
