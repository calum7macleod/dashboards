Five things, the last one's a potential problem

One. What's there
- Ferrari World, SeaWorld, Warner Bros, an arena the size of the O2, and the F1 season finale - Silverstone with a yacht marina
- The hotels were 85% full in August - the month the Gulf's meant to be empty
- The island's bigger than Richmond Park three times over, and 40% of it is still empty land. Here's what's going on it

Two. What's coming
- Disneyland Abu Dhabi, the seventh in the world, around 2030. Disney says Disneyland Paris has put 84 billion euros into France, and is 6% of its tourism income
- A Sphere - the second on earth after Las Vegas - Harry Potter at Warner Bros, and a high-speed rail stop that puts Dubai 30 minutes away from 2031
- Which is why the prices did this

Three. The numbers
- Apartments rose 24% in 2025, the year Disney was announced, and another 18% to June 2026
- The average sale is 560 thousand dollars - about what a three-bed semi costs in Surrey, for a two-bed on an island with a Ferrari rollercoaster
- Yields around 6%, no income tax on the rent - a UK buy-to-let after tax is nearer 3%, which is what drives the next bit

Four. Who's buying
- Aldar's Yas Point launch in July took 400 million dollars in days, and 84% of them had never bought from Aldar before - new money, not the same investors going round
- 25 minutes from the financial district, and the closest island to Dubai. Now the downside

Five. The catch
- 7,700 properties under construction on Yas - more than any island in Abu Dhabi
- 44 million visitors on one side, 7,700 new homes on the other. Whether that balances is the question for the next five years

Yas is the holiday island - apartments here rent to tourists. Next time I'll speak about Saadiyat, it's a bit different. DM ISLAND for the PDF.
