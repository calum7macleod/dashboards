# It's Impossible For Abu Dhabi Supply To Keep Up With Demand - reel (Content draft, verified 4 Nov 2026)

Text: It's Impossible For Abu Dhabi Supply To Keep Up With Demand
Speech: 18 new residents per off-plan property sold is not sustainable - and here's why it can't be fixed quickly

Three things - the last is what gives

One. The gap
- Abu Dhabi added 288 thousand people last year - a city the size of Nottingham, arriving in twelve months. Developers sold 15,900 off-plan homes. Eighteen people for every one
- Dubai added 332 thousand and sold 146 thousand - around two per home
- Even if every new household was a family of four, Abu Dhabi needed four times what it sold. Here's how you know it's real

Two. The proof
- New apartment rents up 17% in a year - 21% on the islands - the regulator's number. Rents only move like that when people can't find somewhere to live
- ADGM added 12,400 jobs this year, up 34% - and the people filling them rent before they buy
- Golf Estates, Yas Point, Bashayer - every launch this year sold out in days. Not because of hype. Because the people were already here
- And the supply side can't speed up

Three. Why it can't catch up
- Around 37,000 homes are being built for delivery by 2030. At three or four people a home, that's a third of one year's arrivals - over four years
- In the first half of 2025 the city finished fewer than a thousand homes. It added 140,000 people
- Abu Dhabi's rules won't let developers build what they haven't sold - that's the brake that stopped a 2009 here, and it's why supply moves at the speed of sales, not demand
- Rents moved first: 17% in a year, 21% on the islands. In June the regulator froze renewal increases - a government saying out loud that supply can't keep up

So something gives. Rents already have. Then prices. The only question is who's holding the property when it does. DM OFFPLAN for info.
