06 · The mortgage route

Off-plan mortgages don't work. Until now.

Every off-plan deal in the UAE has worked the same way: you fund the developer in instalments while it's built, and at handover you either mortgage it or pay the final chunk in cash. Modon has changed that on Hudayriyat, with ADIB - the first off-plan villa mortgage in the country.

  • Your cash stops at 20%. The bank funds the remaining 80% - construction instalments included - and you pay the mortgage, not the developer.
  • Same villa, same island, half the cash of the 40/60 route to reach handover - and none of the 60% cliff at the end.

Why the mortgage route doubles the return

The gain is the same in both routes - it belongs to the villa, not the financing. What changes is the cash it's measured against: Đ3.78M in the 40/60 route, Đ1.98M in the mortgage route. Half the cash, the same gain, twice the return - and the mortgage interest through construction is the price of that leverage.

The honest line: at 6% growth the 40/60 buyer makes 11% a year on cash; the mortgage buyer 19%. At 10% it's 19% and 31%. These are projections, not promises - the register decides.

Growth cases are illustrative. Does not include mortgage interest paid during construction, service charges after handover, or rental income. Abu Dhabi rents are currently frozen by decree, which caps yield growth but not capital growth.
